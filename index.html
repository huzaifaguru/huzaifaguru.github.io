01 — case study

Click-decay scoring for FlyRank

FlyRank ML Internship — Lane 4, CTR / Engagement Opportunity Scoring

§1 the problem

FlyRank tracks 11,119 pages across 28 clients. Reviewing all of them by hand to catch underperformers would take about eleven years. Some pages keep their search rank but still lose clicks compared with other pages getting the same exposure — a decline nobody notices because the rank never moves. The task: find those pages without a human reading through all 11,119.

§2 what I did

  • Built a baseline first, not a model: a hand-written rule that ranks each page against the median click-through rate of pages with similar impression volume, scores the shortfall, and weights it by log impression volume.
  • Tested four learned models against that rule — logistic regression, a depth-3 decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting — instead of assuming a model would win by default.
  • Validated with client-grouped 5-fold cross-validation on 8,825 pseudonymized pages and 44 features, instead of random splits. Random splits underestimated uncertainty by 1.9× to 2.8×, so the weaker statistical power was the honest trade to make.
  • Dropped 90-day CTR aggregates overlapping the outcome window and excluded outcome/production flags, so the model couldn't see the answer before making the call.

§3 what came of it

  • Zero of four models beat the rule at K=50. Left alone, the depth-3 tree re-derived the rule's own logic.
  • Where the models earned their place: reaching 44 of 6,053 low-volume pages the rule structurally couldn't see — it flagged zero of them.
  • 42% of flagged pages recover on their own within 30 days, no fix applied — which is why this project attaches no value estimate to any fix.

Directional, not decisive: rule and model are statistically indistinguishable on precision. The model's real value is reach into low-volume pages. The 42% baseline recovery rate means this is a triage tool, not a fix-and-measure pipeline.
